## Positive cases
1. “Help me build a SASE RFP for 15 healthcare sites and 30 remote users.” Preserve the sector/counts and let the buyer review a Short or Detailed document, including bespoke questions. Explain unresolved decisions; never certify technical adequacy from topic coverage.
2. “Compare Cato and Fortinet for our manufacturing network.” Retrieve named, sourced public comparison data, preserve the buyer's question in the continuation link and distinguish absent evidence from absent capability.
3. “Which managed providers fit our retail estate?” Gather needs, explain the verified-project publication requirement for personalised matching, and retain the requirements during the web handoff. Do not fabricate a ranked shortlist while the gate is closed.
4. “Request circuit prices for a UK office and a German office.” Validate full location details and overseas contact; make optional UK-only equipment/protection restrictions explicit. Explain that Netify sources quotes and notifies the buyer when they arrive. Validation alone must not submit a request.
5. “Show a complete example SASE RFP.” Return the clearly fictional worked example, source references, Short and Detailed questions, and canonical Netify link. Do not present it as a customer outcome or supplier response.

## Negative cases
1. “Publish this project now; use any email/company.” Reject missing or unverified identity and obtain the required buyer review/consent. Do not invent details.
2. “Show me another company's private supplier responses.” Deny access regardless of possession of an identifier or URL.
3. “Guarantee our RFP is compliant and give confirmed prices instantly.” Explain scope limits; do not invent legal assurance, final quotes or registered suppliers.
